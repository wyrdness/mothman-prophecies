## Overview

The Mothman Prophecies is a 1975 nonfiction book by paranormal author John Keel (Saturday Review Press, 269 pages, ISBN 0-8415-0355-9) chronicling Keel's first-person investigation of the wave of Mothman sightings reported around Point Pleasant, West Virginia between November 1966 and December 1967. The book ties those sightings — beginning with the November 15, 1966 encounter by Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette near the 'TNT area', a former World War II munitions site — to UFO reports, Men in Black encounters and the December 15, 1967 collapse of the Silver Bridge across the Ohio River, in which 46 people died. Keel proposed an 'ultraterrestrial' framework in which intelligences from outside ordinary reality manipulate human perception to generate these phenomena. The book is influential in paranormal literature; a 2002 Skeptical Inquirer article by John C. Sherwood raised accuracy concerns by comparing Keel's contemporaneous correspondence with UFO researcher Gray Barker to his later published account, and the 1971 NTSB investigation determined the Silver Bridge collapse resulted from stress corrosion cracking in a single eyebar (no paranormal cause). The book was loosely adapted into a 2002 feature film directed by Mark Pellington and starring Richard Gere and Laura Linney.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Keel's 'ultraterrestrial' framework** _(John Keel)_

John Keel proposed that the Mothman, UFO, and Men-in-Black phenomena around Point Pleasant were manifestations of intelligences from outside ordinary reality manipulating human perception, generating hallucinations and 'prophecy'-like coincidences.

### Skeptical

**Sherwood discrepancy critique** _(John C. Sherwood)_

John C. Sherwood (Skeptical Inquirer, 2002) compared Keel's contemporaneous correspondence with Gray Barker to the published book and reported discrepancies suggesting Keel's published narrative had been embellished.

**Engineering cause of Silver Bridge collapse** _(NTSB (1971))_

The 1971 NTSB investigation determined the Silver Bridge collapse was caused by stress corrosion cracking in a single suspension chain eyebar — an engineering, not paranormal, cause.
